<Section icon={Search} title="How to Read Terminal Clues">

Terminal puzzles in a surreal horror setting often communicate through the environment rather than through a single obvious instruction panel. Before entering numbers, pause and inspect the full space. Check walls, doors, consoles, lights, labels, object groupings, and visual motifs that appear more than once.

The most useful clue is not always beside the terminal. A sequence may be split across a hallway, an adjacent room, a nearby object, or a route that becomes meaningful only after an interaction. Treat the terminal as the final step in a chain of observations.

| Clue type | What to inspect | How to use it |
|---|---|---|
| Repeated symbols | Shapes, icons, marks, or symbols appearing in a pattern | Compare order, direction, and frequency |
| Environmental numbers | Labels, room markings, displays, or written digits | Check whether placement suggests sequence |
| Object arrangement | Items grouped, stacked, or positioned unusually | Test reading order such as left-to-right or nearest-to-farthest |
| Lighting changes | Flickering lamps, colored lights, or illuminated panels | Use changes as attention markers, not automatic numbers |
| Access changes | Doors, routes, or devices activated after objectives | Reinspect the area after progression |

Follow this inspection order:

<Steps>
  <Step num="1" title="Secure the room">
    Clear immediate threats or identify a safe retreat route before investigating the terminal. A rushed interaction can cause missed clues or force you to repeat the search.
  </Step>
  <Step num="2" title="Inspect the terminal itself">
    Read every visible label, symbol, status message, and nearby indicator. Note whether the device appears active, locked, damaged, or incomplete.
  </Step>
  <Step num="3" title="Search the surrounding space">
    Examine walls, corners, side rooms, objects, and alternate routes. Look for repeated details and one-off markings that may define the sequence.
  </Step>
  <Step num="4" title="Test the strongest interpretation">
    Enter the sequence most clearly supported by the environment. Avoid cycling through random combinations, especially when attempts may consume time or resources.
  </Step>
  <Step num="5" title="Record the result">
    Note whether the terminal opens, changes state, rejects the entry, or produces another clue. A response can identify whether the sequence or the timing was wrong.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Callout type="tip" title="Editor’s Method">
When several clues appear possible, rank them by proximity, repetition, and visual emphasis. The most reliable interpretation usually connects multiple details instead of relying on one isolated number.
</Callout>

<Section icon={Map} title="Terminal Route and Backtracking Strategy">

Divine Frequency currently presents Early Access content across the available first two Acts, with exploration built around branching spaces, optional discoveries, blocked paths, and progression checkpoints. That structure makes route management important. A terminal that cannot be solved immediately may be intentionally designed for a later return.

Use a compact route log rather than trying to memorize every unresolved interaction. Record the area, the terminal’s condition, the nearby clue, and the trigger that may change access.

| Record field | Example entry | Why it helps |
|---|---|---|
| Area | Opening corridor, side room, lower route | Identifies the correct return location |
| Terminal state | Locked, inactive, accepts input, unclear | Separates unavailable devices from unsolved puzzles |
| Visible clue | Repeated marks near doorway | Reminds you what to inspect first |
| Progress trigger | After objective completion | Suggests when to revisit |
| Result | No response, rejection, access change | Prevents repeating the same failed attempt |

  <Card title="Essential Route">

  </Card>
  <Card title="Optional Route">
    Explore side rooms after nearby threats are controlled. Optional spaces may contain clues, rewards, or lore details.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Return Route">
    Revisit marked locations after major interactions, new access, or a change in the surrounding environment.
  </Card>
</CardGrid>

A good backtracking routine has three stages:

- **First pass:** identify the terminal, inspect its immediate surroundings, and record unresolved clues.
- **Progression pass:** complete the nearby essential objective without spending excessive resources on guesses.
- **Return pass:** revisit the location with a clearer route, stronger preparation, and any newly available interaction.

This approach also helps distinguish a puzzle from unfinished or inaccessible Early Access content. If a terminal gives no meaningful response and no nearby clue supports an entry, leave it marked rather than assuming the game expects brute force.

<Section icon={ShieldAlert} title="Troubleshooting Failed Terminal Entries">

A failed number entry does not always mean the numbers are wrong. The terminal may require a nearby interaction, a specific progression state, an item, or a return visit after the environment changes. Diagnose the failure before trying another sequence.

| What happened | Likely interpretation | Recommended response |
|---|---|---|
| The terminal stays inactive | It may not be part of the current progression state | Check nearby objectives and return later |
| The entry is rejected | The sequence or reading order may be incorrect | Reinspect the clue pattern and test one supported alternative |
| The environment changes | The interaction may have advanced the puzzle | Search the terminal room and adjacent routes again |
| A route opens | The terminal solution likely worked | Record the location and inspect the newly accessible space |
| Nothing changes after several attempts | The device may be optional, incomplete, or progression-locked | Stop guessing and mark it for a later return |

Use these checks before resetting your route:

- Did you inspect every nearby room, including corners and side paths?
- Did you compare repeated symbols with the one detail that appears only once?
- Did a recent story objective change access or lighting?
- Are you certain the terminal is active in the current Early Access build?
- Did you record the exact response instead of only marking the attempt as failed?
