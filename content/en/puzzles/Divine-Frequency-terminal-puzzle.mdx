<Section icon={Search} title="How to Read the Terminal Clues">

The most reliable way to solve the puzzle is to classify every clue before deciding what it means. Divine Frequency favors surreal visual design, so not every strange object is part of the solution. Give priority to details that are repeated, deliberately framed, or changed after an interaction.

Start with the terminal itself, then expand your search in a controlled circle. Check the room containing the console, the nearest side rooms, and any route that appears to lead toward the same symbol language. Avoid searching the entire level until the local clues have been exhausted.

| Clue Type | Recognition Pattern | Recommended Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Repeated symbol | Appears in multiple locations | Likely part of the puzzle vocabulary |
| Unique symbol | Appears once near a key object | Possible starting point or confirmation marker |
| Changed lighting | Light color or intensity shifts | May indicate an active interaction |
| Object alignment | Items form a visible line or order | May suggest input direction |
| Sound change | Hum, tone, or interference changes | Can confirm a correct state or trigger |

Use the following priority order when several clues seem possible:

1. **Repeated visual marks** near the terminal.
2. **A unique detail** that breaks the local pattern.
3. **Object placement** that creates a readable direction.
4. **Lighting or sound changes** after an interaction.
5. **Decorative details** that do not repeat or connect to the area.

<Section icon={KeyRound} title="Terminal Sequence Method">

Once the clues are collected, convert them into an interaction order. The key question is not simply “Which symbol looks important?” Instead, ask how the game presents the symbols spatially. The intended sequence may follow a path, a visual hierarchy, or the relationship between repeated marks and one unique marker.

A practical rule is to treat repeated symbols as confirmation and the unique symbol as direction. If a symbol appears in several locations, it may tell you which input family is valid. If one symbol appears only once beside a conspicuous object, it may identify where the sequence begins or ends.

| Sequence Question | Correct Test | Common Mistake |
|---|---|---|
| Which symbol comes first? | Find the unique marker or starting point | Choosing the largest symbol |
| What order follows? | Trace physical placement or visual direction | Sorting by appearance alone |
| Is the clue active? | Check for lighting, sound, or state changes | Assuming every decoration matters |
| Is the entry accepted? | Look for objective or environmental feedback | Repeating the same input blindly |
| Is the puzzle complete? | Check the newly opened route or terminal state | Leaving before confirming progression |

### Observation Hint

Look for a detail that appears deliberately isolated from the surrounding pattern. This may be a different symbol, a single light, an unusual panel, or an object positioned at the edge of an otherwise repeated arrangement.

### Second Hint

Compare the isolated detail with the route between the clue locations. The order may be read from the entrance toward the terminal, from the terminal outward, or along a visible left-to-right or high-to-low arrangement.

### Full Method

Use the unique environmental marker to establish the starting point, then follow the repeated symbols in the direction suggested by their placement. Enter the sequence only after each symbol has a clear reason to occupy its position. If the terminal accepts the input, confirm the resulting door, panel, light, or objective update before moving away.

<Section icon={ShieldAlert} title="Mistakes, Resets, and Nearby Dangers">

Terminal puzzles are often placed near optional encounters, blocked routes, or rooms that punish rushed exploration. Before interacting, make the area safer. Clear a nearby path when practical, identify the closest retreat route, and avoid spending rare healing or ammunition simply to investigate a suspicious console.

If the terminal resets, treat the reset as information. Check whether one symbol was entered in the wrong position, whether the puzzle requires a prior environmental interaction, or whether a nearby clue was overlooked. A reset does not necessarily mean the entire puzzle must be rediscovered.

| Situation | Likely Cause | Recommended Response |
|---|---|---|
| Terminal does nothing | Required interaction is incomplete | Search nearby rooms and return after checking objectives |
| Sequence resets | Input order is incorrect | Rebuild the sequence from spatial clues |
| Door remains closed | Puzzle state was not confirmed | Look for a switch, sound, light, or objective update |
| Enemy interrupts | Optional encounter was triggered | Retreat, recover resources, and return with a clear route |
| Clues seem inconsistent | Decorative details were included | Prioritize repeated and deliberately framed elements |
| Area feels unfinished | Early Access progression limit | Record the location and continue with available objectives |

A careful player should also distinguish between three different outcomes:

- **Input rejection**: The terminal refuses the selected symbol or sequence.
- **Puzzle reset**: The interface returns to its starting state.
- **Successful activation**: A door, light, sound, panel, or objective changes.

Only the third outcome confirms that the terminal puzzle is complete. A satisfying sound or visual effect may indicate progress, but always check the surrounding area for the actual route forward.
