<Section icon={Target} title="First-Time Setup and Progression Route">

The opening areas reward a methodical routine rather than rushing from encounter to encounter. Divine Frequency combines shooting, exploration, resources, optional routes, and RPG-style progression, so a strong first session should establish reliable habits before experimenting with specialized builds.

<Steps>
  <Step num="1" title="Learn the combat rhythm">
    Start by testing how firearms and psionic options behave at different distances. Use cover, maintain an escape route, and avoid spending every powerful resource during the first exchange. When entering an unfamiliar room, identify nearby doorways or obstacles before committing to combat.
  </Step>
  <Step num="2" title="Map accessible and blocked routes">
    Explore side rooms before advancing through major objectives when the area is safe. Remember locked doors, inaccessible paths, unusual rooms, and containers that may become relevant later. Landmarks are especially useful when the environment contains branching routes.
  </Step>
  <Step num="3" title="Protect your resource reserve">
    Treat ammunition, healing, upgrade materials, and psionic resources as one survival budget. Spend them when they solve a clear problem, but preserve enough capacity for required encounters and progression blockers.
  </Step>
  <Step num="4" title="Choose one primary damage plan">
    Build around one dependable weapon or ability category first. Add a secondary tool that covers the primary plan’s weakness instead of spreading upgrades across several unrelated options. Reliability is more valuable than early specialization.
  </Step>
  <Step num="5" title="Record unresolved objectives">
    Keep a short list of doors, optional rooms, suspicious objects, and unexplored branches. Return after major story progress or when a new ability changes how you approach the area. Early Access content can also change between updates.
  </Step>
</Steps>

| Priority | What to do | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Essential | Identify exits and cover before combat | Reduces the risk of being trapped |
| Essential | Check side routes before advancing | Helps reveal resources and optional content |
| Recommended | Save high-impact abilities | Preserves options for dangerous encounters |
| Recommended | Note blocked paths | Makes later backtracking faster |
| Situational | Attempt optional fights | Do so when resources support the risk |

<Callout type="tip" title="Exploration Rule">
Complete the main objective in an area before spending excessive resources on every optional threat. A marked return route is often more valuable than an expensive first attempt.
</Callout>

</Section>

<Section icon={Swords} title="Combat Tools, Builds, and Resource Management">

The available guidance supports three broad approaches: firearms-focused, psionics-focused, and hybrid play. These are useful planning categories rather than confirmed class names or fixed character classes. Start with the approach that matches your preferred combat distance, then use the second system to solve its most common weakness.

| Build direction | Main role | Main resource | Recommended opening plan |
|---|---|---|---|
| Firearms-focused | Reliable ranged damage | Ammunition | Improve consistency, accuracy, handling, and damage efficiency |
| Psionics-focused | Control, utility, or burst effects | Psionic resources | Use a small ability set and carry a firearm for sustained damage |
| Hybrid | Adaptable combat coverage | Shared resource pool | Choose one primary damage method and use the other as support |
| Defensive utility | Safer exploration and recovery | Healing and ability reserves | Prioritize escape options and resource preservation |

A practical loadout should answer three questions before entering an unfamiliar area:

- Can you handle a target at your preferred range?
- What happens if enemies close the distance?
- Which resource will be hardest to replace during the next encounter?

Avoid upgrading every available option immediately. A focused setup makes ordinary encounters easier to read and gives you a clearer basis for deciding which modifications are worth keeping.

<Callout type="success" title="Build Recommendation">
For a first run, use one dependable firearm, one flexible psionic or utility option, and upgrades that improve consistency. Add specialized effects only after you understand which situations cause the most trouble.
</Callout>

<Section icon={Shield} title="Enemy Preparation and Optional Encounters">

Enemy encounters are easier to manage when you prepare the room before triggering danger. The most useful habits are observing movement, preserving retreat routes, and separating normal combat from high-risk optional events.

| Encounter type | Main danger | Efficient response | Preparation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Early-act hostiles | Close-range pressure | Create distance and use doorways | Keep basic healing and a reliable weapon |
| Fast pursuers | Rapid approach and cornering | Break line of sight and retreat through cleared areas | Keep doors and escape routes accessible |
| Special-preparation encounters | Tight spaces, hazards, or repeated pressure | Inspect the area and reserve strong resources | Restore healing, clear nearby objectives, and identify retreat paths |
| Mixed exploration combat | Changing range and uncertain threats | Use a balanced loadout | Avoid entering with an empty resource reserve |

Before activating a suspicious object or entering a conspicuous arena:

- Check nearby rooms for supplies.
- Identify a route back to a safer area.
- Confirm that your primary weapon has enough ammunition.
- Preserve healing for mistakes rather than minor damage.
- Search the encounter space afterward for rewards or lore details.

Optional content should not automatically be treated as mandatory progression. If a route appears too expensive or inaccessible, mark it and continue with the current objective. Returning later is often more efficient than forcing an unfavorable encounter.

<Section icon={Sparkles} title="Puzzles, Secrets, and Early Access Expectations">

Surreal horror environments often communicate through repeated visual details, sound changes, unusual object placement, and spaces that appear deliberately framed. When a puzzle is not immediately clear, compare nearby rooms and look for the detail that appears only once.

Use a gradual hint process:

1. Inspect markings, symbols, lighting changes, and object placement.
2. Compare repeated details with unique environmental details.
3. Revisit the area after completing nearby story objectives.
4. Search newly accessible branches before leaving the region.
5. Treat complete solutions as a last step, especially when preserving discovery matters.

The current Early Access structure also affects how you should read the game’s content. Some planned systems, enemies, bosses, story elements, music, and Hardcore Mode features are intended for later development rather than guaranteed features in the current build. Keep that distinction in mind when comparing player reports or planning a long-term playthrough.

| Content status | Meaning for players |
|---|---|
| Available | Present in the current Early Access build |
| Released update | Added or changed through an official patch |
| Planned | Described as future development, not currently guaranteed |
| Community report | Useful context, but not a substitute for official patch notes |

For updates, use the [Steam Community hub](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2548880/), [official Discord](https://discord.gg/jnyFVSTgf3), and verified developer channels. These are the best places to check whether a progression issue, balance change, or content update applies to the build you are playing.
