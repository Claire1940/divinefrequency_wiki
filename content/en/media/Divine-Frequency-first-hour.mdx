<Section icon={Map} title="Divine Frequency First Hour Roadmap">

The **Divine Frequency first hour** is less about rushing through objectives and more about establishing a repeatable survival routine. The current Early Access build contains the opening portion of the planned campaign, so careful navigation and resource discipline are more valuable than trying to force every locked route immediately.

Use the first hour to learn how the game communicates danger, progression, and optional content. Check rooms before advancing, keep an escape route in mind, and record anything that appears inaccessible. This approach reduces backtracking and gives you a clear return list when the story or progression system opens new possibilities.

| Time | Main Priority | What to Do |
|---|---|---|
| 0–10 minutes | Basic controls | Test movement, aiming, interaction, inventory, and available combat options. |
| 10–20 minutes | Safe exploration | Check side rooms, identify landmarks, and note blocked paths. |
| 20–35 minutes | Combat rhythm | Practice spacing, cover, retreat routes, and resource conservation. |
| 35–50 minutes | Progression | Compare upgrades and focus materials on one dependable combat plan. |
| 50–60 minutes | Route review | Confirm the current objective, revisit useful branches, and record unresolved areas. |

<Steps>
  <Step num="1" title="Test every essential control">
    Before entering unfamiliar rooms, confirm movement, aiming, interaction, weapon switching, and any available ability inputs. Early mistakes are easier to correct in a safe area than during a pressured encounter.
  </Step>
  <Step num="2" title="Build a simple mental map">
    Identify doors, corridors, landmarks, and obvious exits. If a route is blocked, remember its location instead of repeatedly testing it during the same pass.
  </Step>
  <Step num="3" title="Establish a combat fallback">

  </Step>
  <Step num="4" title="Spend resources with a purpose">
    Use ammunition, healing, abilities, and upgrade materials when they solve a clear problem. Keep a reserve for required encounters and progression blockers.
  </Step>
  <Step num="5" title="Review before advancing">

<Section icon={Swords} title="Early Combat and Enemy Management">

The opening encounters reward observation and positioning. Instead of attacking continuously, watch how enemies approach, where they recover after an attack, and which rooms give you enough space to maneuver. A reliable retreat is often more valuable than a few extra seconds of damage.

Use firearms for controlled ranged pressure when distance is available. Keep psionic or utility options available for situations that demand crowd control, unusual access, burst damage, or a response that does not depend entirely on ammunition. The strongest early loadout is usually a focused combination rather than several partially upgraded tools.

<CardGrid cols={3}>
  <Card title="Control Distance">
    - Fight from a range that gives you time to react.
    - Avoid backing into unknown rooms.
    - Use doorways and corners to limit pursuit.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Read Recovery Windows">
    - Watch for missed attacks.
    - Strike after an enemy commits.
    - Retreat when an attack chain becomes difficult to read.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Protect Your Reserve">
    - Save healing for unavoidable danger.
    - Avoid using rare abilities on isolated targets.
    - Keep ammunition for groups or progression encounters.
  </Card>
</CardGrid>

| Encounter Type | Main Risk | Efficient Response | Resource Priority |
|---|---|---|---|
| Close-range hostile | Being trapped in a confined room | Create distance, use corners, and attack after recovery | Preserve healing and ammunition |
| Fast pursuer | Losing access to a safe retreat | Break line of sight and move through cleared spaces | Save emergency recovery options |
| High-pressure encounter | Limited space or repeated waves | Inspect the area, prepare first, and reserve strong tools | Use the strongest option only when needed |
| Unknown encounter | Triggering multiple threats | Identify exits before interacting with suspicious objects | Keep a balanced reserve |

When entering a new room, pause briefly and identify three things: the likely threat direction, the safest retreat path, and the nearest piece of cover or environmental protection. This small check helps prevent panic spending and makes unfamiliar spaces easier to read.

<Callout type="warning" title="Avoid Panic Spending">
Do not use every powerful tool in the first exchange simply because an enemy appears dangerous. If the room has space and a retreat route, test the encounter before committing rare resources.
</Callout>

<Section icon={Shield} title="Exploration, Resources, and Upgrades">

Exploration in Divine Frequency works best when you separate essential progression from optional investigation. Main objectives should move your current route forward, while side rooms, strange environmental details, and blocked passages can be added to a return list.

The Early Access structure makes this especially important. Some areas may require later access, a different interaction, or a progression change that is not available during the first visit. Avoid treating every inaccessible location as a mistake in your route.

| Resource | First-Hour Use | Recommended Rule | Common Mistake |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ammunition | Sustained firearm damage | Spend it on threats that block progress or create real risk | Clearing every optional enemy immediately |
| Healing | Recovering from serious damage | Keep a reserve before entering unknown areas | Using it for minor damage after safe encounters |
| Psionic resources | Utility, control, or special damage | Save high-impact effects for groups or difficult targets | Treating every ability as a basic attack |
| Upgrade materials | Improving weapons or abilities | Strengthen one reliable plan first | Dividing materials across too many options |
| Time and route knowledge | Finding objectives and returns | Mark landmarks and unresolved branches | Repeating the same search without notes |

A good first upgrade should improve consistency. Look for benefits that make ordinary encounters easier, improve handling, increase practical efficiency, or support the range you use most often. Highly specialized upgrades can be useful later, but they are harder to evaluate before you understand the game’s encounter variety.

| Upgrade Direction | Best For | Strength | Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Firearm consistency | Direct ranged combat | Predictable damage and simple planning | Depends on ammunition |
| Psionic utility | Flexible responses | Helps with control, access, or unusual encounters | Limited by ability availability |
| Defensive support | Careful exploration | Gives more room for mistakes | May reduce immediate damage |
| Hybrid setup | Mixed encounters | Covers changing ranges and threats | Reaches specialization more slowly |

<Callout type="success" title="Upgrade Rule">
Choose one primary damage method and use your second system to cover its weakness. This focused approach is easier to manage than upgrading every available option at once.
</Callout>
