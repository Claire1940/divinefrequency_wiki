<Section icon={Swords} title="Combat Tips: Win Fights Without Wasting Supplies">

Combat becomes more consistent when you treat enemy behavior as information. Do not attack simply because a target is visible. Watch how it approaches, identify its recovery window, and decide whether the safest answer is damage, repositioning, or disengagement.

For close-range threats, create space before attacking again. Corners, doorways, and cleared rooms can help control pursuit. Fast enemies are especially dangerous when they push you against scenery, so breaking line of sight may be more valuable than forcing a direct exchange.

<Steps>
  <Step num="1" title="Read the Encounter">
    Identify how many enemies are present, which target creates the most pressure, and where you can retreat. Avoid stepping deeper into an unknown area while under attack.
  </Step>
  <Step num="2" title="Choose the Cheapest Reliable Tool">
    Use a dependable firearm or basic ability when it can solve the encounter safely. Save rare healing, high-impact powers, and specialized ammunition for situations that justify the cost.
  </Step>
  <Step num="3" title="Control Distance">
    Back away before enemies reach striking range. Use doorways, corners, and previously cleared spaces to prevent pursuit from becoming a swarm.
  </Step>
  <Step num="4" title="Attack Recovery Windows">
    Strike after an enemy misses, finishes a chain, or becomes temporarily exposed. Repeated attacks during its active pressure phase can turn a manageable fight into a resource drain.
  </Step>
  <Step num="5" title="Reset Before the Next Room">
    Reload, check your health, and confirm your escape route before continuing. Do not carry panic from one encounter into the next.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Callout type="warning" title="Avoid Panic Spending">
Using every powerful option at the start of a fight can leave you vulnerable to the next threat. If an enemy can be controlled with positioning and ordinary damage, keep your strongest tools in reserve.
</Callout>

| Encounter Type | Main Risk | Efficient Response | Reserve For |
|---|---|---|---|
| Early-Act hostile | Close-range pressure | Create distance and use recovery windows | Healing if retreat is blocked |
| Fast pursuer | Being trapped against scenery | Break line of sight and use obstacles | Emergency defense or healing |
| Confined group | Multiple attacks at once | Control the approach through a doorway | High-impact ability |
| Special-preparation encounter | Hazards, waves, or limited space | Inspect the area and prepare first | Strongest weapon or defensive option |

Use firearms for reliable ranged pressure, but do not depend on one damage method in every situation. Psionic options can provide control, utility, or burst potential, while a firearm remains useful when abilities are unavailable or better saved.

</Section>

<Section icon={Map} title="Exploration, Puzzles, and Resource Management">

Exploration in Divine Frequency is safer when you separate the main route from optional content. Complete the immediate objective, record blocked access points, and return when the area is less dangerous or a new interaction changes the route. This approach protects your supplies while still rewarding curiosity.

Side rooms deserve attention, but they do not all need to be cleared immediately. A suspicious sound, repeated symbol, unusual lighting change, or conspicuous empty space may signal an optional discovery. Examine the environment before interacting with a mechanism, especially when the area suggests a hidden event.

<Callout type="info" title="Use a Return List">
Keep a short list of locked doors, inaccessible branches, unusual symbols, and unresolved rooms. Revisit the list after completing a major objective instead of searching the entire map from memory.
</Callout>

| Exploration Signal | Recommended Action | Common Mistake |
|---|---|---|
| Locked route | Record its location and continue | Spending supplies trying to force access |
| Repeated symbol | Compare nearby rooms and objects | Treating every visual detail as decoration |
| Optional side room | Check it when resources allow | Entering without an escape route |
| Environmental change | Revisit earlier locations | Assuming the first route remains unchanged |
| Unusual sound or space | Prepare before interacting | Activating an event while depleted |

Resource management should cover more than ammunition. Healing, psionic availability, upgrade materials, and inventory space all contribute to survival. Before spending, ask whether the purchase improves ordinary encounters, solves a known weakness, or only supports a narrow situation.

<Section icon={Target} title="Progression, Builds, and Upgrade Priorities">

A focused build is easier to use than several partially developed options. Start with one reliable damage method, then add a secondary system that covers its main weakness. Firearms-focused play favors consistent ranged pressure, psionics-focused play emphasizes utility and active resource decisions, and hybrid builds provide broader coverage at the cost of slower specialization.

<Callout type="success" title="Recommended Upgrade Order">
Improve reliability first, then add specialization. Damage consistency, handling, resource efficiency, and emergency coverage usually provide more value early than a narrow bonus that only helps one encounter type.
</Callout>

| Archetype | Strength | Weakness | Beginner Direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Firearms-focused | Reliable ranged damage | Ammunition dependence | Improve consistency and keep one utility option |
| Psionics-focused | Flexible control and burst | Ability resource limits | Save strong effects and carry a firearm |
| Hybrid | Adaptable encounter coverage | Slower specialization | Choose one primary damage method first |
| General framework | Clear spending decisions | Requires patience | Add a second role after the first is stable |

Choose upgrades according to the problems you actually face:

- If ordinary enemies consume too much ammunition, prioritize consistency and efficiency.
- If enemies frequently close the distance, add control, defensive, or mobility support.
- If exploration encounters vary heavily, use a hybrid setup with one dependable firearm and one flexible psionic option.
- If a specialized build feels weak, stop adding new categories and strengthen the core tool first.

<Callout type="tip" title="Build Planning Rule">
The best early build is not the one with the highest theoretical damage. It is the one that solves common encounters without exhausting the resources needed for the next objective.
</Callout>

The current Early Access version is still developing, so avoid treating planned systems or future content as available features. Keep your setup easy to adjust, especially when entering a new chapter or returning after a major update.

</Section>
