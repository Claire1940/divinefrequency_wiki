<Section icon={Map} title="Exploration, Puzzles, and Hidden Areas">

Exploration in Divine Frequency rewards observation more than speed. Unusual markings, repeated symbols, lighting changes, object placement, and isolated environmental details can signal that a room deserves a closer inspection.

When a puzzle is unclear, use a progressive hint process. First compare the surrounding environment, then identify repeated or unique details, and only afterward test the relevant interaction. This method reduces random input attempts and helps preserve resources for combat.

| Discovery Type | What to Inspect | Best First Action |
|---|---|---|
| Environmental clue | Symbols, markings, lighting, object placement | Compare repeated details with unique details |
| Hidden route | Side rooms, blocked sightlines, unusual walls | Revisit after nearby objectives change |
| Interactive mechanism | Switches, objects, sequence markers | Look for a visual order before activating |
| Optional encounter | Strange sound, restricted space, repeated motif | Identify a retreat route and prepare supplies |
| Lore discovery | Unusual text, isolated objects, hidden rooms | Search the surrounding area after the main task |

Use the following puzzle routine:

- Observe the entire room before interacting.
- Identify details that appear more than once.
- Look for one unique symbol, object, or lighting change.
- Check whether a nearby story interaction altered the environment.
- Return to the mechanism only after understanding the likely sequence.
- Search the area again after solving the interaction.

<Callout type="info" title="Spoiler-Controlled Exploration">
Start with environmental observation and light hints. Open a full solution only after checking repeated symbols, unusual object placement, nearby rooms, and newly changed routes.
</Callout>

The same logic applies to hidden areas. A side path may become meaningful only after completing the immediate story requirement. Instead of clearing every branch on the first visit, finish the main objective, remember the location, and return with a more suitable loadout.

A practical exploration record can include:

- Room or landmark name
- Door, mechanism, or route that needs another condition
- Nearby objective that may affect access
- Resources required for a possible encounter
- Whether the location appears essential or optional

This approach is particularly useful while the Early Access campaign is still being expanded. It lets you distinguish current progression from future content without treating every unresolved detail as a failure.

<Section icon={Swords} title="Combat Preparation and Resource Management">

The opening areas favor deliberate positioning and consistent resource management. Firearms provide reliable ranged pressure, while psionic options can add utility, control, unusual access, or burst damage. A focused hybrid loadout is often easier to manage than several partially upgraded tools.

| Combat Tool | Primary Role | Resource Concern | Recommended Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Firearms | Reliable ranged damage | Ammunition | Use for controlled engagements and sustained pressure |
| Psionic options | Utility, control, or burst | Ability availability | Save for dangerous targets, groups, or special situations |
| Weapon modifications | Build specialization | Upgrade materials | Improve one clear combat plan before broad experimentation |
| Hybrid loadout | Flexible encounter coverage | Shared resource reserve | Pair one dependable weapon with one complementary ability |

Before entering an unfamiliar room:

- Identify at least one retreat route.
- Check whether nearby doors remain open.
- Keep enough ammunition for a required encounter.
- Avoid spending rare healing on isolated threats when retreat is possible.
- Reserve high-impact abilities for groups, dangerous targets, or progression blockers.
- Reconsider your setup when the area begins demanding a different range or response.

<Section icon={Shield} title="Act Progression and Backtracking Rules">

The available campaign is structured around the first two Acts, with essential and optional content best handled as separate routes. Complete the immediate story requirement before attempting a costly side encounter, then return when the area is easier to read and your equipment is better prepared.

| Situation | Immediate Response | Return Condition |
|---|---|---|
| Main route is clear | Advance while preserving supplies | Revisit nearby branches after the objective |
| Door is inaccessible | Record its location and clues | Return after a major story or access change |
| Optional area is dangerous | Withdraw and preserve resources | Return with healing and a balanced loadout |
| Objective appears stalled | Review completed interactions and prior branches | Check earlier unresolved routes |
| New area increases pressure | Reassess weapons and abilities | Continue when the build covers the encounter range |

The most reliable backtracking cycle is:

1. Complete the nearest essential objective.
2. Confirm that the story or route state has changed.
3. Search the immediate area for useful resources.
4. Add unresolved paths to a short return list.
5. Revisit only when the required condition or equipment is available.

This prevents a common Early Access mistake: spending too long trying to solve a route that is not currently required. It also keeps optional discoveries meaningful, because you can investigate them with enough supplies to survive the encounter rather than entering underprepared.

The game is currently presented as an English-language Early Access release, and its available content may change through updates. For that reason, keep progression notes tied to locations and objectives instead of relying only on a fixed sequence of room names. Check the [Steam Community hub](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2548880/) for current announcements and player discussion when a new build changes behavior.

<Callout type="tip" title="Backtracking Rule">
Advance the story when the required route is available, but keep a short list of unresolved locations. Return after meaningful progression instead of repeatedly searching every branch.
</Callout>

For the current four-to-six-hour Early Access scope, efficient exploration matters more than clearing every room on the first pass. The goal is to finish essential progression with enough resources to investigate optional content and adapt to later encounters.
