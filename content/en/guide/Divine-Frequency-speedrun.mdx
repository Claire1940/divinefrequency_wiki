<Section icon={Target} title="Step-by-Step Speedrun Setup">

Before timing serious attempts, establish a repeatable opening routine. Early runs should teach you where time is lost: combat, navigation, resource collection, or hesitation. Do not optimize every room at once. Lock down one segment, then move to the next.

  <Step num="1" title="Define the Current Endpoint">

  </Step>
  <Step num="2" title="Create a Safe Opening Route">
    Move through the opening area while identifying the main objective, nearby exits, cover, and blocked access points. The goal is to remove hesitation without spending unnecessary resources.
  </Step>
  <Step num="3" title="Test Combat Options">
    Try firearm and psionic tools during early encounters. Keep the option that produces the most consistent progress for your route rather than selecting a setup based only on theoretical damage.
  </Step>
  <Step num="4" title="Record Segment Splits">
    Mark the time when you leave major areas, complete essential objectives, and reach important progression checkpoints. These splits reveal where a reset is actually costing time.
  </Step>
  <Step num="5" title="Remove Low-Value Detours">
    Compare each optional room against its travel time, combat risk, and reward. Keep the detour only when it reliably improves survival, access, or future speed.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Section icon={Swords} title="Combat and Loadout Choices">

A speedrun loadout should reduce decision-making during ordinary encounters. The available guidance supports three broad approaches: firearms-focused, psionics-focused, and hybrid play. Each can work as a practice foundation, but a hybrid setup is often the safest starting point because it covers changing distances and resource pressure.

Do not split upgrades across every available option. Choose one primary damage method, then use the second system to solve its most important weakness. This keeps the build understandable while preserving an emergency answer for difficult rooms.

| Loadout Style | Primary Role | Main Risk | Speedrun Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Firearms-focused | Reliable ranged damage | Ammunition dependence | Strong for predictable target elimination |
| Psionics-focused | Utility, control, and burst effects | Ability resource limits | Useful when abilities bypass difficult encounters |
| Hybrid | Flexible coverage | Slower specialization | Good starting point for route discovery |
| Defensive hybrid | Survival and controlled progress | Lower damage pressure | Useful for learning high-risk sections |

<CardGrid cols={4}>
  <Card title="Firearms">
    Use for controlled ranged pressure and encounters where distance is safer than close combat.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Psionics">
    Save utility or high-impact effects for dangerous targets, blocked routes, or grouped threats.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Positioning">
    Use doorways, corners, cover, and cleared rooms to control how enemies approach.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Resource Reserve">
    Keep enough healing, ammunition, and ability capacity for the next required encounter.
  </Card>
</CardGrid>

The most useful combat habit is to avoid fighting in unknown spaces. Before entering a room, identify a retreat route and nearby cover. If a fast pursuer or close-range hostile forces you into a bad position, breaking line of sight may save more time than attempting an immediate kill.

| Encounter Type | Preferred Response | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Close-range pressure | Create distance and attack after recovery windows | Fighting while trapped against scenery |
| Fast pursuer | Retreat through cleared spaces and use obstacles | Running into an unexplored room |
| High-pressure area | Prepare resources and inspect the arena first | Activating the encounter without an escape plan |
| Isolated target | Use the cheapest reliable option | Spending rare abilities unnecessarily |

<Callout type="success" title="Combat Rule">
The best speedrun attack is the one that ends the encounter without forcing a recovery detour. Prioritize clean positioning and repeatable damage over flashy risk.
</Callout>

<Section icon={Shield} title="Route Discipline and Reset Strategy">

Speedrun progress depends on knowing when to continue and when to reset. A single missed interaction does not always justify abandoning an attempt, especially while learning. However, repeated navigation errors, severe resource loss, or a failed mandatory encounter can make a reset more efficient.

Use checkpoints to separate route mistakes from execution mistakes. A route mistake means the planned path itself is inefficient or unclear. An execution mistake means the route is sound, but combat, movement, or interaction was handled poorly. These require different fixes.

/>

| Problem | Continue When | Reset When |
|---|---|---|
| Minor damage taken | Healing remains adequate for required encounters | Damage creates a later resource crisis |
| Missed side room | The room is optional and not route-critical | The missed interaction blocks progression |
| Extra ammunition spent | The next section remains manageable | A mandatory fight cannot be supported |
| Navigation error | You can return without major backtracking | The mistake invalidates the planned route |
| Failed encounter | The recovery path is short and repeatable | The attempt loses more time than a restart |

The current build rewards deliberate exploration, so speed does not mean ignoring every environmental clue. The correct approach is to distinguish between clues required for progression and details that support optional lore or discoveries. If an interaction does not advance the active objective and has no known route value, postpone it during a timed attempt.

<Callout type="tip" title="Reset Tip">
Reset for information, not frustration. After each failed attempt, write down the exact cause and change one decision in the next run.
</Callout>
