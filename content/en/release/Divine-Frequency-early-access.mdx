<Section icon={Target} title="Step-by-Step Beginner Route">

The opening areas reward deliberate exploration more than speed. Use the following route to establish a reliable routine before experimenting with specialized builds or optional encounters.

<Steps>
  <Step num="1" title="Learn the Combat Rhythm">
    Test both firearm and psionic options during safe encounters. Use cover, control the distance to hostile targets, and avoid spending every powerful resource during the first exchange. When entering an unfamiliar room, identify nearby cover and at least one retreat route before committing to combat.
  </Step>
  <Step num="2" title="Explore in a Consistent Pattern">
    Check side rooms before advancing through major objective areas. Pay attention to doors, paths, containers, and interactive objects that cannot be opened yet. Use landmarks and completed objectives to avoid searching the same spaces repeatedly.
  </Step>
  <Step num="3" title="Reserve the Survival Budget">
    Ammunition, healing supplies, upgrade materials, and psionic resources should be treated as one connected budget. Spend resources when they solve a clear problem, but keep a reserve for required encounters and progression blockers.
  </Step>
  <Step num="4" title="Choose One Primary Damage Plan">
    Start with one dependable firearm or psionic approach. Add a complementary option only after the primary setup feels reliable. A focused early build is easier to manage than several partially upgraded systems.
  </Step>
  <Step num="5" title="Record Unresolved Paths">
    Do not assume every blocked route is a bug or that every optional room must be cleared immediately. Write down inaccessible areas, unusual symbols, locked doors, and unexplained interactions, then return after major story progress.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Section icon={Swords} title="Combat, Weapons, and Build Priorities">

Divine Frequency supports multiple combat approaches rather than a single required loadout. Firearms provide dependable ranged pressure, psionic options add flexibility and utility, and modifications help specialize a preferred plan.

The safest beginner setup is usually a focused hybrid: select one reliable damage source, then use the second system to cover its main weakness. This keeps your resource spending understandable while preserving options for changing encounters.

| Combat Approach | Main Strength | Main Risk | Recommended Early Focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| Firearms-focused | Consistent ranged damage | Ammunition dependence | Handling, accuracy, consistency, and ammo efficiency |
| Psionics-focused | Flexible damage and utility | Ability availability and resource timing | Effect strength, recovery, duration, and efficiency |
| Hybrid | Broad encounter coverage | Upgrade materials spread too thin | One primary system plus one complementary tool |
| Modification-focused | Strong specialization | Narrow build flexibility | Bonuses that reinforce the same range or damage plan |

<CardGrid cols={3}>
  <Card title="Firearms Priority">
    Use firearms for controlled ranged engagements and sustained damage. Pair them with a utility or defensive psionic option instead of investing equally in every available system.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Psionic Priority">
    Save high-impact abilities for dangerous targets, groups, unusual encounters, or situations where conventional ammunition is inefficient.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Hybrid Priority">
    Maintain one dependable firearm, one useful psionic option, and modifications that improve the same combat rhythm rather than unrelated effects.
  </Card>
</CardGrid>

A practical upgrade order is:

- Improve consistency before chasing narrow specialization.
- Compare the immediate benefit of an upgrade against saving its materials.
- Keep an emergency option available for fast enemies or confined rooms.
- Reassess your build when a new area changes engagement ranges or resource pressure.
- Avoid committing to several expensive options before you understand your preferred combat role.

<Callout type="success" title="Build Recommendation">
For a first run, prioritize reliability over maximum specialization. A focused primary weapon or ability combined with one flexible backup is easier to use across exploration-heavy areas.
</Callout>

</Section>

<Section icon={Shield} title="Exploration, Secrets, and Resource Control">

Exploration is a major part of the current Early Access experience. The game’s surreal environments can contain branching routes, optional encounters, environmental clues, and areas that become meaningful only after later interactions.

Use a low-spoiler method before looking for direct solutions:

1. Inspect repeated symbols, unusual markings, lighting changes, and conspicuous object placement.
2. Compare nearby rooms to identify details that appear only once.
3. Revisit earlier spaces after a major story interaction or new traversal opportunity.
4. Prepare healing and combat resources before activating a suspicious object.
5. Search the encounter area again after combat for rewards, lore items, or newly accessible exits.

| Discovery Type | First Check | Follow-Up Action |
|---|---|---|
| Blocked route | Door, passage, or object that cannot be used | Record its location and return after story progress |
| Environmental puzzle | Repeated symbols or unusual object placement | Compare nearby rooms and identify the unique clue |
| Hidden area | Side room, blind corner, or changed route | Revisit after completing the nearby objective |
| Optional encounter | Strange sound, restricted space, or visual motif | Prepare an escape route and reserve combat resources |
| Lore discovery | Unusual text, object, or environmental detail | Inspect the surrounding area before leaving |
