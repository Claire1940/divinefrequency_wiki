<Section icon={Swords} title="Beginner Combat and Exploration Setup">

The opening areas reward deliberate movement more than constant aggression. Divine Frequency combines ranged combat, dangerous spaces, and exploration routes that can branch or remain inaccessible until later. A simple routine helps prevent wasted ammunition, unnecessary damage, and repeated backtracking.

<Steps>
  <Step num="1" title="Learn the Combat Rhythm">
    Start by testing how firearms and psionic options behave at different ranges. Use cover, keep space from unfamiliar enemies, and avoid spending every powerful resource during the first exchange. The safest tool is usually the one that lets you control the encounter rather than the one with the highest immediate output.
  </Step>
  <Step num="2" title="Identify Exits Before Engaging">
    Before entering an unfamiliar room, look for doorways, corners, open paths, and nearby cover. Knowing where to retreat is especially valuable when an encounter introduces a fast pursuer or limits your movement.
  </Step>
  <Step num="3" title="Explore Side Rooms Methodically">
    Check optional branches before advancing too far through a major objective. If a door or route cannot be opened, record its location and continue instead of spending excessive resources searching for an immediate solution.
  </Step>
  <Step num="4" title="Preserve a Resource Reserve">
    Ammunition, healing, upgrade materials, and psionic resources should be treated as one survival budget. Keep enough capacity for an unavoidable fight, a progression blocker, or a return through a dangerous area.
  </Step>
  <Step num="5" title="Review Your Route at Checkpoints">
    After a major objective, confirm that the story has advanced and note unresolved locations. This creates a short return list and reduces the chance of repeatedly searching the same rooms.
  </Step>
</Steps>

| Situation | Recommended response | Common mistake |
|---|---|---|
| Unknown room | Identify cover and retreat routes first | Entering without an escape plan |
| Isolated enemy | Use controlled damage and preserve rare supplies | Spending high-impact abilities immediately |
| Fast pursuer | Break line of sight and move through cleared areas | Retreating into an unexplored route |
| Locked path | Record the location and continue | Assuming every blocked route must open now |
| High-risk encounter | Prepare healing, equipment, and space | Activating the encounter while underprepared |

<Section icon={Shield} title="Weapons, Psionics, and Build Planning">

Divine Frequency supports more than one combat approach. The most practical way to plan a build is to choose one dependable primary damage method and use a secondary system to cover its weakness. This prevents a common early mistake: investing evenly in every available option and ending up without a reliable answer for routine encounters.

| Combat approach | Main strength | Main limitation | Useful pairing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Firearms-focused | Consistent ranged damage | Ammunition dependence | Psionic control or utility |
| Psionics-focused | Flexible effects and encounter control | Resource timing and recovery | Reliable firearm |
| Hybrid setup | Coverage across ranges and situations | Slower specialization | One primary tool plus one support tool |
| Modification-focused | Greater build specialization | Upgrade materials may be limited | A focused weapon or ability |

<CardGrid cols={3}>
  <Card title="Firearms Focus">
    Prioritize dependable damage, handling, accuracy, and ammunition efficiency. This is a straightforward choice for players who prefer distance and readable combat patterns.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Psionics Focus">
    Save high-impact abilities for dangerous targets, groups, or unusual situations. Keep a firearm ready for sustained encounters when ability resources are limited.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Hybrid Focus">
    Select one primary damage source, then use the second system to solve its most important weakness. Upgrade reliability before adding narrow specialization.
  </Card>
</CardGrid>

A practical loadout should answer three questions:

- Can it handle a normal encounter without excessive resource spending?
- Can it respond when an enemy closes distance or changes the arena?
- Does it leave enough reserve for exploration, healing, and progression?

<Callout type="info" title="Build Planning Rule">
Start with reliability, then add specialization. A focused weapon or ability setup is easier to evaluate than several upgrades that do not support the same range or combat plan.
</Callout>

When comparing modifications, consider effect strength, recovery, range, resource efficiency, and how well the option complements your main tool. A strong secondary option does not need to duplicate your primary damage. It should instead provide control, emergency coverage, mobility, or a way to handle encounters that punish your usual approach.

</Section>
