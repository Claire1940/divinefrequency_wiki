<Section icon={Swords} title="Divine Frequency doom Combat Fundamentals">

In this Divine Frequency doom guide, “doom” describes an aggressive retro-FPS approach rather than a separate game mode or confirmed crossover. Divine Frequency combines horror FPS combat, RPG progression, surreal exploration, and resource decisions. The strongest opening habit is to stay active without turning every encounter into a reckless rush.

Use movement to control the fight. Enter unfamiliar rooms with an exit in mind, identify nearby cover, and avoid standing in a confined space while several threats can approach. When an enemy closes the distance, reposition first and attack during a recovery window instead of trading damage without a plan.

| Combat Priority | Recommended Habit | Common Mistake |
|---|---|---|
| Positioning | Identify cover and retreat routes before engaging | Entering rooms without an escape path |
| Distance | Keep enemies in a range your current weapon handles well | Allowing fast threats to trap you |
| Resource use | Save high-impact tools for groups or dangerous targets | Spending every ability on minor enemies |
| Exploration | Check side paths and remember blocked doors | Repeating the same area without landmarks |
| Recovery | Attack after missed strikes or recovery animations | Firing continuously without observing patterns |

<Section icon={Target} title="Step-by-Step Divine Frequency Doom Setup">

A reliable setup does not require immediate specialization. Start with one dependable damage method, then add a secondary tool that covers its main weakness. Firearms provide consistent ranged pressure, while psionic options can add utility, control, unusual access, or burst potential.

Follow this sequence during the opening areas and adjust it as new encounters reveal what your current setup lacks.

<Steps>
  <Step num="1" title="Test Both Combat Systems">
    Try firearm and psionic options early so you understand their range, timing, and resource demands. Do not commit every upgrade material before identifying which tool feels most reliable in ordinary encounters.
  </Step>
  <Step num="2" title="Choose One Primary Damage Role">
    Select either firearms, psionics, or a balanced hybrid as your initial focus. A clear primary role makes early upgrades more meaningful and prevents several partially developed options from competing for resources.
  </Step>
  <Step num="3" title="Add a Complementary Emergency Tool">
    Choose a secondary option that solves the main problem created by your primary choice. A firearm can support a resource-limited ability setup, while a psionic tool can provide utility when conventional ranged damage becomes unsafe.
  </Step>
  <Step num="4" title="Upgrade Reliability Before Specialization">
    Prioritize improvements that make ordinary encounters more consistent. Accuracy, handling, effect strength, recovery, and resource efficiency are often more useful early than narrow bonuses for rare situations.
  </Step>
  <Step num="5" title="Reassess After New Areas">
    Change your setup when a new section introduces different ranges, enemy pressure, or exploration demands. Early Access progression may also change as the game receives updates during 2026.
  </Step>
</Steps>

| Setup Style | Main Strength | Main Risk | Best Starting Plan |
|---|---|---|---|
| Firearms-focused | Reliable ranged damage and readable combat | Ammunition pressure and weaker close-range flexibility | Improve consistency, then add utility support |
| Psionics-focused | Flexible damage, control, and special utility | Ability availability and resource timing | Save high-impact effects for dangerous encounters |
| Hybrid | Broad coverage across ranges and threats | Slower specialization and higher planning demands | Choose one primary damage source first |
| Defensive utility | Safer retreats and stronger encounter control | Lower immediate damage output | Pair with a dependable weapon or ability |

<Callout type="success" title="Build Rule">
A focused build is usually easier to use than several equal investments. Make one combat option dependable before expanding into a second specialization.

</Callout>

Use landmarks to remember where you have been. Side rooms, locked access points, optional encounters, and unusual environmental details can become important later. If a path is blocked, record it mentally or in notes and continue with the active objective instead of spending all your resources searching for an immediate solution.

| Side room | Supplies, environmental clues, alternate paths | When the area becomes safer or access changes |
| Locked route | Door location, visual clue, nearby interaction | After major progression or new ability access |
| Optional encounter | Retreat route, healing, available cover | When your loadout can support the risk |
| Puzzle space | Repeated symbols, unusual objects, lighting changes | After examining surrounding rooms |

<Section icon={Shield} title="Resource Management and Enemy Preparation">

The best doom-style approach is aggressive in movement but conservative with resources. Ammunition, healing, upgrade materials, and psionic resources form one shared survival budget. Spending one resource should solve a clear problem: a dangerous target, a blocked route, a group encounter, or a failed retreat.

Early-Act hostiles can often be handled by creating distance and using corners or doorways. Fast pursuers deserve more caution because they become dangerous when they force you against scenery. Special-preparation encounters should be approached only after checking the arena, nearby exits, and available supplies.

| Threat Pattern | Key Danger | Efficient Response | Preparation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Close-range pressure | Repeated attacks in confined spaces | Create distance and attack after recovery | Keep a retreat route open |
| Fast pursuit | Rapid closing distance and corner pressure | Break line of sight and use obstacles | Clear nearby routes first |
| Special encounter | Limited space, hazards, or repeated waves | Inspect the arena and reserve strong tools | Carry healing and your best equipment |
| Mixed pressure | Changing ranges and multiple priorities | Use a balanced loadout and reposition often | Avoid entering with an empty reserve |

<CardGrid cols={3}>
  <Card title="Firearms">
    Use firearms for dependable ranged damage, controlled target elimination, and sustained pressure when ammunition is available.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Psionic Options">
    Save flexible abilities for crowd control, unusual situations, burst damage, or moments when conventional attacks are unsafe.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Hybrid Loadout">
    Combine one consistent firearm with one useful psionic option, then choose modifications that support the same combat plan.
  </Card>
</CardGrid>

<Callout type="tip" title="Resource Check">
Before entering an unfamiliar room, ask three questions: What is my escape route? Which tool handles the likely threat? What resource must remain available afterward?

</Callout>
