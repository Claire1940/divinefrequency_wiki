<Section icon={Swords} title="Combat Setup and Resource Management">

The most reliable early approach is to build around consistency rather than immediately chasing a highly specialized loadout. Firearms provide dependable ranged damage, while psionic options can offer utility, control, burst damage, or solutions for unusual encounters.

A balanced setup does not require equal investment in every system. Instead, choose one primary damage method and use the second system to cover its main weakness. This keeps ammunition, healing, upgrade materials, and psionic resources manageable during the opening chapters.

<Callout type="warning" title="Avoid Early Resource Waste">
Do not spend rare ammunition, healing, or high-impact abilities on every minor threat. Keep a reserve for progression blockers, groups, and encounters that limit movement.
</Callout>

| Combat Category | Main Role | Best Early Use | Upgrade Priority |
|---|---|---|---|
| Firearms | Reliable ranged damage | Open rooms, controlled target elimination, distance management | Consistency, accuracy, handling, ammunition efficiency |
| Psionic options | Damage, control, and utility | Groups, unusual threats, burst windows, non-ammunition solutions | Effect strength, recovery, duration, resource efficiency |
| Weapon modifications | Build specialization | Reinforcing a preferred range or encounter plan | Complementary bonuses over unrelated effects |
| Hybrid loadout | Flexible encounter coverage | Mixed enemy pressure and exploration-heavy sections | Reliability first, specialization second |

<Steps>
  <Step num="1" title="Test both combat systems">
    Use early encounters to compare firearms and psionic options. Identify which tool feels dependable at range, which handles pressure, and which resource each system consumes.
  </Step>
  <Step num="2" title="Choose one primary damage plan">
    Select a firearm-focused, psionics-focused, or hybrid direction. A clear priority makes early upgrades more valuable than spreading materials across every option.
  </Step>
  <Step num="3" title="Reserve emergency resources">
    Keep healing, powerful abilities, and high-value ammunition available for difficult rooms or encounters that prevent easy retreat.
  </Step>
  <Step num="4" title="Add coverage for weaknesses">
    If your primary plan struggles at close range, add control or defensive utility. If abilities are limited, retain a dependable firearm for sustained damage.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Section icon={Shield} title="Exploration, Levels, and Puzzle Habits">

Divine Frequency rewards deliberate exploration. Opening areas may contain branching paths, locked access points, optional encounters, resources, and environmental clues. A consistent route through each location is more useful than repeatedly wandering through every room.

Before entering an unfamiliar space, identify nearby cover and a possible retreat route. After completing a major objective, revisit earlier branches rather than assuming that every blocked route is unfinished progression. Some paths may be designed for later access, while others may contain optional discoveries.

<Callout type="success" title="Exploration Rule">
When a route is blocked, record its location and move on. Return after a story interaction, new ability, or progression change instead of repeatedly spending resources on an inaccessible path.
</Callout>

| Exploration Task | Recommended Action | Why It Helps |
|---|---|---|
| Entering a new room | Check exits, cover, and visible threats | Prevents rushed retreats and accidental resource loss |
| Finding a locked route | Mark its location mentally or in notes | Makes later backtracking faster |
| Completing a major objective | Recheck nearby side paths | Access or environmental conditions may have changed |
| Investigating unusual scenery | Inspect markings, symbols, lighting, and object placement | These details may provide puzzle clues |
| Preparing for an optional encounter | Clear nearby objectives and identify an escape route | Reduces the risk of being trapped with unfinished tasks |

Environmental puzzles should be approached in stages:

- Look for repeated symbols, unusual markings, and deliberately placed objects.
- Compare nearby rooms to identify details that appear only once.
- Treat recurring visual motifs as possible sequence markers.
- Revisit the relevant mechanism after a major interaction changes the area.
- Reveal a complete solution only after checking the surrounding environment.

<Section icon={Target} title="Enemy Preparation and Walkthrough Priorities">

Early enemy encounters reward observation, spacing, and positioning. Close-range hostiles become more manageable when you create distance and use doorways or corners to control their approach. Fast pursuers are more dangerous when they force you against scenery, so breaking line of sight can be safer than immediately trading attacks.

High-pressure encounters deserve preparation before activation. Inspect the surrounding area, confirm that nearby objectives are complete, and keep your strongest equipment available for the encounter's most dangerous phase.

<Callout type="tip" title="Field Tactics">
When an enemy has a clear recovery window, use that moment to attack. Otherwise, prioritize distance, line-of-sight breaks, and movement through spaces you have already cleared.
</Callout>

| Threat Type | Main Risk | Efficient Response | Preparation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Early-Act hostiles | Close-range pressure in confined rooms | Create distance and use corners or doorways | Enter with an identified exit |
| Fast pursuers | Rapid approach and attack chains | Break line of sight and attack after a miss | Keep traversal routes open |
| Special-preparation encounters | Limited space, hazards, or repeated waves | Separate threats and reserve powerful tools | Bring healing and strongest equipment |
| Unknown encounter | Unclear attack pattern or arena layout | Observe before committing resources | Inspect the area and confirm retreat options |

For the current Early Access walkthrough, separate essential story progression from optional content. Complete the active objective, record unresolved paths, and return to optional areas when your equipment and resources can support the risk.

The two available chapters should be treated as evolving content. Early Access changes may affect balance, progression blockers, performance, or the location and behavior of encounters. Check the latest [Steam community announcements](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2548880/allnews/) before beginning a new run after a major update.

</Section>

<Section icon={Sparkles} title="Build Planning and Early Access Expectations">

A strong beginner build is simple, repeatable, and easy to adjust. The best early upgrades are usually those that make ordinary encounters more consistent rather than those that only improve a narrow situation.

Players who prefer direct combat can emphasize firearms and use psionic tools for emergencies or utility. Players who enjoy flexible abilities can emphasize psionics while retaining a firearm for long engagements. A hybrid setup can cover more situations, but it may reach full specialization later and requires more careful spending.

<Callout type="info" title="Early Access Reminder">
Planned features are not the same as currently playable features. Treat the existing two-chapter build as the active reference and verify future additions through official announcements.

| Build Direction | Strengths | Weaknesses | Beginner Recommendation |

| Psionics-focused | Strong utility, control, and encounter flexibility | Ability resources require careful timing | Save high-impact effects for dangerous targets |
| Hybrid | Broad coverage across ranges and threat types | Slower specialization and higher material demand | Choose one primary damage source |
